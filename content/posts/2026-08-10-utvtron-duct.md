[Last time](/2026/07/27/utvtron/) I said the next ride was the duct. Sunday afternoon I took it.. 2017 RZR 4 900, three ThermoPro tips this time. The app handed back a share card that looked done.

![utvtron share card for Aug 9 · Afternoon: 96°F max, 90°F avg, 18 min, 9.0 miles, 3 impacts, three probe series](/uploads/2026/08/utvtron-aug9-share.png)

96° max, 18 minutes, 9 miles. Green line flat, blue line doing all the work. I almost posted that and called it a day.

Then I opened the CSV.

## The Hose I Could See

The clutch housing is in the left-rear wheel well, and a fat J-shaped hose clamps onto the round cover. That's the hose I could see, so that's where a tip went. One stayed in the cabin. One went in the engine bay, since that's the only place that got hot last time.

![CVT intake hose on the 2017 RZR 4 900, clamping onto the outer clutch cover](/uploads/2026/08/utvtron-cvt-intake.png)

In the app I named them Belt and Ambient, and the third one just ended up as "Probe 3."

## What the CSV Said

708 rows, a reading every second and a half. Bluetooth held, the screen stayed awake, no timestamp pileup like the first ride.

"Belt" sat around 90°. Probe 3 sat around 90°. "Ambient" started at 106° and hit **163°** fifteen minutes in. The card only showed the first probe, so the headline was 96° while the actual heat was on the channel I'd named Ambient. I had the plugs backwards.

## That Hose Is the Intake

The J-tube is the **CVT intake**.. it pulls cool air *in*. A tip in there is just going to read cabin air, which is exactly what it did. The clutches are further inboard, not behind that rubber.

And on this generation there's no clutch exhaust grill. Polaris dumps the hot air right **into the engine bay**, over the trans, at the block and header. So the bay probe is a mix of engine heat and whatever the clutch was blowing out. Still not a belt reading.

![ThermoPro TP25 on the RZR floor, one cable routed through the floor toward the clutch](/uploads/2026/08/utvtron-thermopro-floor.png)

I wasn't going to build GPS or multiple probes this early. Built them anyway, and they paid off.. three lines on one chart is what made the mixup obvious.

Next tip goes in the hose that *leaves* the clutch, and this time the names match the plugs.
